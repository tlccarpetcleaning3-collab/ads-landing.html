## The First 5 Minutes Matter Most

When your dog or cat has an accident on the carpet, what you do in the first few minutes determines whether the stain comes out easily or becomes a permanent problem.

### Do This Immediately:

1. **Blot with a clean white cloth or paper towels.** Press firmly and lift. Repeat with fresh sections until you're not picking up moisture.
2. **Apply cold water** (not hot — heat sets protein stains like urine).
3. **Blot again** from the outside edges toward the center to prevent spreading.
4. **Place a stack of paper towels on the spot** with something heavy on top and leave for a few hours to absorb what's deep in the pad.

### Never Do This:

❌ **Don't rub or scrub.** This pushes the stain deeper into the carpet fibers and pad, and can damage the carpet texture permanently.

❌ **Don't use hot water.** Heat bonds protein stains to carpet fibers, making them nearly impossible to remove.

❌ **Don't use bleach.** It will remove the stain — along with your carpet's color.

❌ **Don't use strong-scented sprays to mask the odor.** If you can still smell it, so can your pet, and they'll keep going back to the same spot.

## Why Store-Bought Products Usually Fail

Most products at the store — Resolve, OxiClean, even the "pet enzyme" sprays — work on the surface. But here's the problem: **urine soaks through the carpet into the pad and even the subfloor underneath.**

That spray you bought treats the top half-inch of carpet. The urine is 2 inches deep. The stain looks gone, but the bacteria and odor-causing crystals are still down there, breaking down and producing that unmistakable ammonia smell — especially on humid days.

This is why pet stains seem to "come back" after you clean them.
